# Optimisation Algorithms
*Academic Year 2024 - 2025*  
*Semester 1*

# NUSMods Description
> This course covers common algorithmic techniques for solving optimisation problems, and introduces students to approaches for finding good-enough solutions to NP-hard problems. Topics covered include linear and integer programming, network flow algorithms, local search heuristics, approximation algorithms, and randomized algorithms. Through analysis and application of the techniques to a variety of canonical problems, students develop confidence to (i) appropriately model a given optimisation problem, (ii) apply appropriate algorithmic techniques to solve the problem, (iii) analyse the properties of the problem and candidate algorithms, such as time and space complexity, convergence, approximability, and optimality bound.

# Review
This class was taught by Prof Divesh Aggrawal and consists of the topics: Vertex Cover, Set Cover, Linear Programming, Travelling Salesman Problem, Constraint Satisfaction Problems, Lovasz Local Lemma, Semidefinite Programming, Network Flow problems and Applications, Simplex Method and Ellipsoid method and other randomisation problems. The graded components include scribing, 2 graded assignments and then tutorial participation and the Final exam.

According to Prof Divesh, this is probably the "hardest algorithms class you will experience in NUS". Certainly true! Despite the more advanced nature of this class as compared to previous iterations under Prof Halim, I think this really gives a good introduction to tackling computationally hard problems (NP-HARD) by designing approximate (and sometimes randomised) algorithms to give solutions that are good enough, and provably so! The lectures are quite fun, sometimes Prof Divesh writes in cursive so its a bit hard to copy down notes properly. There are weekly scribes (it is 10\% of the grade), so we need to make good notes which are circulated amongst the class for revision and learning. Scribing was done in LaTex which was ok for me as I am experienced in it. GPT is a good tool if you suck at drawing diagrams or graphs.

There were also weekly problem sets which are discussed during the tutorial session. Problem sets are somewhat difficult and we were given the chance to present our solutions during the tutorial for participation points. No solutions were released to promote discussion and participation. However, it really depends on the quality of the tutor. Prior to Week 7, the graduate TA for this class was pretty trash and the tutorials were not very useful because he just mumbles too much and hence very unclear. There was a mid-week feedback and I think some people criticised the graduate TA too much such that the Prof took over entirely (which I am VERY happy about). Prof Divesh really cares about our learning and encourages us to share as many solutions as possible, even if it is wrong. I started to open up as a result and participated in discussions even more.

The 2 graded homeworks is insanely hard. It takes like maybe 5 days to finish each of them. I keep revising my solutions and proofs to a level of quality I am satisfied with. Prof also said that marking would be a headache and he certainly "asked for it" after realising some proofs are very advanced. But it is a good practice and the questions are interesting (that is what it should be!). I did relatively well for the homework. The marks which I lost is because I became lazy towards the end and half-assed some proofs.

Prof also promised that Finals would be 6 easy questions which I think is good as exams should test how well you can apply certain principles instead of relying on some external instinct/intuition. Well, it is open-book exam and thus it kind of is less on memorisation already. I think I accidentally messed up 1 question's proof which caused me to loose my distinction grade.. Haish the sadness of overcomplicating a solution.

All in all, I am so blessed that I took this class under Prof Divesh. He is such a great prof who is caring and enthusiastic about the student's learning. I hope he keeps teaching the class. This is exactly (!!!) how Optimisation Algorithms should be taught.
