# Discrete Optimisation
*Academic Year 2024 - 2025*  
*Semester 1*

# NUSMods Description
> Discrete optimisation deals with problems of maximising or minimising a function over a feasible region of discrete structure. These problems come from many fields like operations research, management science and computer science. The primary objective of this course is twofold: (a) to study key techniques to separate easy problems from difficult ones and (b) to use typical methods to deal with difficult problems. Major topics: Integer programming: cutting plane techniques, branch and bound enumeration, partitioning algorithms, the fixed charge and plant location problems. Sequencing and job-shop scheduling. Vehicle routing problems.

# Review  
This class was taught by Prof Jonathan Scarlett. He has previously taught me Information Theory the semester prior to this. This class consisted of a midterm, final and a project. I think this class is not too difficult for me given that I am taking CS4234 Optimisation Algorithms in the same semester, so revising for one certainly helps to reinforce the other. Topics included: MILP formulation, Convexity and Polyhedra, Unimodularity and Total Unimodularity, Approximating Solutions, Submodularity, Matroids, Greedy Solutions, Complexity, Global Optimisation methods.

Lecture delivery was the clear. Prof Scarlett writes on his iPad and sort of reads off from the lecture notes. I attended his first 2 lectures live but switched to remote Zoom lectures instead. Well, I say remote. It is just I prefer to sit in the 5-th floor study space and listen to the lecture, which is a few steps from his office. Sometimes I get there early, I see Prof Scarlett walking out of his office to conduct the lecture HAHA. His voice can be quite monotonous, so I sometimes played subtle background music from Studio Ghibli Orchestra so I do not fall alseep. I was able to concentrate much better this way.

I used to skip tutorials, but I do the tutorial worksheets promptly and on time. But after Prof Scarlett threatened to cancel the Zoom option if the attendance gets too low, I got scared and attened the actual live slot. However, on hindsight, the live session seems to be much better. The tutorial worksheets can get very challenging but it was quite fun to do.

There was also a project (with minimal coding required) using Matlab pertaining to MAXSAT. Having studied SAT for a long time, I completed the compulsory parts of the project within 2 days (5 hours) and left the optional part to my own timing (they are actually semi-optional as you can get bonus marks of it). If you do not do the optional, you can only get a maximum of 22/25 which kinda makes the optional part non-optional. The scores for the project are not that differentiating too.

The midterm (haish) I hate exams. I think I struggled at first due to the anxiety. I skipped the first easy questions to do the harder ones first and only to slap myself in the realisation that I found the easy questions to be quite trivial and that I panicked because of it. I did pretty well. Prof Scarlett likes to ask "Give an example of (some thing i.e. ILP, Polyhedra etc.) such that it satisfies (some property)" which I tend not to like. Without the natural intuition, some of my friends tried bruteforcing. For me, I just drew alot of diagrams until I got something. My pro-tip is to think of the simplest case possible and try not to overcomplicate things. Same for the finals. Finals is a 100 marks and last 2.5 hours. I remember being super exhausted at the 2nd hour but then I realised there is 30 minutes left. I think it was not too difficult. I just hope I do well.

Overall, I would prefer this class over the other Math Profs' iterations as this class tests you on concepts instead of computation. From the Past year papers, it seems like one has to spend a lot of time doing Dual and Primal Simplex Method when one asks you to solve ILP using Gomory Cuts - which leads to carelessness. For Prof Scarlett's exams, calculators are not allowed, which is great!
