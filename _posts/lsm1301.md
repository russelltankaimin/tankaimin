# General Biology
*Academic Year 2024 - 2025*  
*Semester 1*

# NUSMods Description
> This is an introductory course that explores what a living thing is, the basics of life, and the science behind it. The course will introduce the chemistry of life and the unit of life. The question of how traits are inherited will be discussed and the field of biotechnology, including its applications and the ethical issues involved be will introduced. The diversity of life on earth will be explored, with discussions how life on earth possibly came about and how biologists try to classify and make sense of the diversity. The course will also introduce the concept of life functions from cells to tissues and from organs to systems. The concept of how organisms maintain their internal constancy and organisation of major organ systems will be discussed. The focus will be to introduce the unifying concepts in biology and how they play a role in everyday life.

# Review
This class is taught by Prof Henry Mok and Dr Zeehan Jaafar. I am clearly a Bio-idiot. On the very first lecture, it was said that this is a very beginner level Biology class for "people who don't necessarily have a background in Biology". Context: I have not done Biology in the past 10 years and Chemistry in the past 6 years. However, I was still very motivated to learn something and make the best out of the situation despite being forced by the School of Computing to take this class as a Science Requirement. My world turned upside down on the first lecture as I was bombarded by a string of new vocabulary at a machine gun rate. The first part of the class was on Cell and Molecular Biology which was a massive pain in the *ss. Every lecture I ended up learning at least 50 new words. It was done at such breakneck speed that the lecturer Prof Henry lost me within 15 minutes. It does'nt help that his accent made me 10000x confused. Sorry it might sound bad but it is my honest opinion and I tried my best to navigate around that situation. When he was teaching DNA and Hereditary, he kept mentioning "FIPINE and TEEPINE", I'm like what is that??? After searching through the notes...I realised he met 5' (5-PRIME) and 3' (3-PRIME) of the DNA strand. 

This course was fully CA. We had 4 labs, 2 tutorials, a field trip and 2 CA term tests. For the lectures, it was split into 2 parts: Molecular and Cell Biology (lectured by Prof Henry), and then followed by Ecology and Biodiviersity (lectured by Dr Zeehan Jaafar) For the CA tests, I actually tried my best to study despite being lost entirely in the lectures. It is hard to picture all the mechanisms (like the metabolic processes to form ATP which gives you energy). For CA1 I had the fortune of the Recess Week to study the notes ultra-carefully and I felt quite confident. Hence, I did better than expected for CA1 (Above median). For CA2, I was unlucky since Week 13 I had 4 finals altogether (seriously what is the point of Exam Week then if you are going to schedule finals on the final week of term?) so my revision was pretty rushed and I failed just by a bit. I would say for the topics of CA2 they were quite nice because its something we can observe around us (Ecology and Biodiversity) but alas I did not do so well for that as I panicked. Both CAs are open book but I don't recall myself needing to flip through anything for CA1, and only sometimes for CA2.  

For the other components - I don't know how despite my best efforts I just could not score anything above median or average. I am unsure what I was lacking in my work. For the lab reports they asked stuff they clearly wanted us to Google instead of testing our critical thinking (why shrimps have this part of their body etc.). I did not want to take the shortcut so I put my intuition and brain to the test and hypothesis certain reasons why certain things work certain ways. Efforts don't really pay off here. Likewise for tutorials. As for the field trip, me and my other SoC friend taking the class planned an optimal route to get all our information needed so we can do it fast and move on (we were already very busy that week).

My expected grade is probably an S (Recall I was trying for an A but maybe I was fighting a losing battle from the beginning). Ok, but I don't regret what I have done and all the efforts I put in. I genuinely wanted to learn (even though my friend did'nt want to and encouraged me not to), but it did not turn out to be a nice experience. See, I am stubborn. For SoC students having no choice but to take this class - only take it if you have prior Chemistry experience otherwise you are better off taking Physics (if they still exist for you), or Forensic Sciences.
