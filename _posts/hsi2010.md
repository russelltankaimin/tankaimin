# New Worlds Beyond Earth
*Academic Year 2024 - 2025*  
*Semester 1*

# NUSMods Description
> The development of space technology during the mid-20th century ushered in a new age of discovery in which outer space is explored for advancing scientific research, commercial use, and ensuring the future survival of humanity. This course illustrates the use of scientific method to study Solar System objects and their physical characteristics, particularly the presence of water and potential biomarkers, and the multiple considerations required in the development of technology for launching artificial satellites, space tourism, asteroid mining, deflecting a potentially hazardous asteroid, and space colonization.

# Review
This class is taken to fufill the 2nd Scientific Interdisciplinary pillar of the College of Humanities and Sciences Curriculum (CHS). This class has been taught from the very beginning by Dr Cindy Ng Shao Chin. The graded components includes 2 term tests, a discussion forum, weekly quizzes and a group project.  

I think the lectures were interesting (as in the content), but the delivery I believe could have been much better. The lecturer is pretty monotonic and it is very hypocritical to expect us to put up a broadcaster voice when recording our project presentations. I believe she brings in a prop for lecture once to show us something about the phases of Venus too (but it was just once - I thought perhaps she would put more effort in the lectures with more props). Eventually, I would just camp in the Medicine-Science Library with my friend (not taking this class) and watch the Zoom version of the lecture and doing other things. As the for Friday lectures, I watched it on Zoom at home - and hence an early weekend!  

Quizzes were quite easy for me. Some may complain that the wordings can be confusing but it did not affect me at all. Dr Cindy allows up to 10% of mistakes so I was'nt that worried. I think most people gotten the full points for this component anyway.

For the project component, we were required to find an article and work in a group of 3 to present the history or event, the scientific method carried out during said event and scientific interdisciplinary aspects of the event related to space travel. I don't think I have any issues from the project as I got to meet new friends.  

For the CA term tests, each test consists of 35 MCQ and 5 questions of 2-parts each which were open ended. Regardless of the format, it is just a memorisation fest. In preparation of each term test, my brain had to hold 420 ppt slides of information (EACH!) and it got to the point I pretty much trained my photographic memory because I would be able to tell you the sequence of x slides and visualise what each slide exactly look like. For the first CA term test I got 37/45 which was like 75 percentile performance out of 60 or so people. The second test I lost quite a bit of steam because I had 3 exams prior that week and tried to just yolo. Amazingly I improved on my score and got a 39/45 (which is still 75 percentile performance).  

What I really learnt to hate is the discussion forum. At first it was interesting to discuss various aspects of a space article but then it slowly became a chore because I felt there were more important things to do. But I still enjoy replying to people and reading more in it. The first part of the discussion only required us to either post something or reply a friend for 2 marks but in the second part, we are not required to post (1 mark) and reply a friend (to get the full 2 marks) which can get very annoying. Some topic requirements were abit confusing. For the "Phospine on Venus" discussion, even after reading the articles, I had nothing to agree or disagree on because there was no point of contention? I was confused.  Anyway, I ended up writing something that I hoped was useful in starting a discussion.

Overall it is a pretty chill class. Your weekly workload is just do the quiz + discussion forum + a little bit of the project which is considered light for me. I would reccoment this class to anyone interested in Astronomy but only if you have a somewhat Physics brain (kinda helped me understand things faster or even skip the lectures.)
