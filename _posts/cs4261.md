# Algorithmic Mechanism Design
*Academic Year 2024 - 2025*  
*Semester 1*

# NUSMods Description
> Recent years have seen a dramatic rise in the use of algorithms for solving problems involving strategic decision makers. Deployed algorithms now assist in a variety of economic interactions: assigning medical residents to schools, allocating students to courses, allocating security resources in airports, allocating computational resources and dividing rent. We will explore foundational topics at the intersection of economics and computation, starting with the foundations of game theory: Nash equilibria, the theory of cooperative games, before proceeding to covering more advanced topics: matching algorithms, allocation of indivisible goods, and mechanism design.

# Review
This class is taught by Prof Warut Suksompong. Algorithmic game theory is commonly split into Multi-agent decision making and mechanism design, so this course covers the latter. Topics include Nash Equilibria, autions + VCG mechanisms, routing games, collaborative games, stable matching, rent division, cake cutting, fair division and tournaments.

The assessment style is as follows: 30% are for weekly assignments, 20% each for Midterms 1 and 2, 30% for Midterm 3. It is a relatively low workload class but with a very tight exam duration.

I like this class because the content is very interesting. The lecturer speaks clearly and I attend all his lectures despite it being at 6.30pm. It was quite interesting to see how we can design mechanisms such that it can be fair, pareto optimal, efficient, and even strategy-proof (no one can gain any more utility by lying). I like studying for this class and enjoy the assignments since they were challenging enough and fun to think about. However, I hate the exams. There were 3 midterms and I scored median scores for my first 2 midterm and close to 75 percentile for the last. It was mostly a computation exam (answer only, no working) with 1 proof question (2 for Midterm 3) and we were given 45 minutes (80 minutes for Midterm 3) to complete the midterm. This resulted in me and my friends rushing through and creating a whole bunch of stupid careless mistakes. For example, for 1 question: I somehow calculated the case of an extra imaginary player, another case my friend wrote 6 * 3 = 19 and during the final midterm, dear me, my friend calculated the Shapely value instead of the payoff vector in the core. Hence, I felt that despite enjoying the class, my grade might not show how much I actually understood the content. 45 minutes went by like a blur when your brain is trying to compute answers at breakneck speed. I can now compute Nash Equilibrium for a 4 x 4 normal games in 3 minutes flat (try me.)

Overall, I reccommend this class for those interested in mechanism design or Algorithmic Game theory in general. I am just warning you about the exam style but I guarantee you that you will see algorithms in a different light after this.
